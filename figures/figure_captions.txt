Figure 0  |  fig0_model_architecture.{pdf,png,svg}
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Block diagram of the MHCBAN model architecture. The network comprises two
parallel BiLSTM encoder branches and a central BAN + MLP decoder.

Left branch (PeptideBiLSTM, 662,656 params): Peptide sequence (max 30 aa,
vocabulary size 25) → Embedding (25→128) → Dropout(p=0.2) → BiLSTM Layer 1
(input=128, hidden=128 per direction, bidirectional output=256) → BiLSTM
Layer 2 (input=256, hidden=128, bidirectional output=256) → feature matrix
(batch × 30 × 256).

Right branch (MHCBiLSTM, 662,656 params): MHC 34-position pseudo-sequence
(NetMHCpan definition, vocabulary size 25) → identical BiLSTM structure →
feature matrix (batch × 34 × 256).

Center — BAN Layer (397,574 params, identical to DrugBAN): v_net FC 256→768
+ ReLU (197,377 params); q_net FC 256→768 + ReLU (197,377 params); h_mat
weight-normalized parameter (attention glimpses = 3, 2,308 params); AvgPool1d
(kernel=3); BatchNorm1d(256) (512 params). Output: attended joint
representation (batch × 256).

MLP Decoder (462,337 params, identical to DrugBAN): FC1 256→512 + BN +
ReLU; FC2 512→512 + BN + ReLU; FC3 512→128 + BN + ReLU; FC4 128→1 (raw
logit). Sigmoid converts logit to binding probability ∈ [0, 1]; threshold
0.5 defines the binder/non-binder decision boundary.

Total parameters: 2,185,223 (all trainable). Parametric layers: 15.
Total computational depth: 21 operations (including activations and dropout).
Color code: purple = input, light blue = embedding, dark blue = BiLSTM,
light green = BAN sub-components, orange = MLP sub-components, dark red = output.

Figure 1  |  fig1_auroc_overview.{pdf,png,svg}
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
AUROC comparison across all 13 MHCBAN experiments (mean ± SD, n = 10 seeds).
Bars are color-coded by training strategy: blue = Random split (Plan 1), orange =
Cluster split (Plan 1), purple = Cross-HLA Domain Adaptation (Plan 2, CDAN),
green = Transfer Learning (Plan 3), red = Pan-HLA universal model (Plan 4).
Dashed horizontal line marks the random classifier baseline (AUROC = 0.5).
The Pan-HLA Random model achieves the highest single-model AUROC (0.979 ± 0.001),
matching individual HLA-A Random performance while covering all three HLA types.

Figure 2  |  fig2_metrics_heatmap.{pdf,png,svg}
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Comprehensive performance heatmap for all 13 experiments across six evaluation
metrics: AUROC, AUPRC, F1, Sensitivity, Specificity, and Accuracy (mean ± SD,
n = 10 seeds). Cells are colored by the RdYlGn colormap (red = low, yellow =
medium, green = high; range 0.3–1.0). Row labels are color-coded by experimental
group (same scheme as Figure 1). Cells with mean < 0.42 or > 0.88 use white text
for readability. Note that HLA-C Cluster shows low AUROC (0.403 ± 0.066) but
high specificity (0.984 ± 0.021), reflecting the model's strong non-binder
recognition but failure to identify binders from unseen alleles.

Figure 3  |  fig3_random_vs_cluster.{pdf,png,svg}
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Side-by-side comparison of Random split versus Cluster (allele cold-start) split
performance for HLA-A, HLA-B, and HLA-C individual models (Plan 1). Paired bars
show mean ± SD across 10 seeds for all six metrics. The performance gap between
Random and Cluster splits reflects the difficulty of generalizing to unseen HLA
alleles: minimal for HLA-A (AUROC Δ = 0.048), moderate for HLA-B (AUROC Δ =
0.194), and severe for HLA-C (AUROC Δ = 0.470), attributable to HLA-C's limited
allele diversity (11 alleles total, only 2 in the test set).

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Figure 4  |  fig4_cold_start_comparison.{pdf,png,svg}
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Method comparison for allele cold-start and universal prediction scenarios (AUROC,
mean ± SD, n = 10 seeds). Four subpanels: (left to right) HLA-A cold-start, HLA-B
cold-start, HLA-C cold-start, and universal prediction across all HLA types.
For HLA-B cold-start, Plan 3 fine-tuning (0.768 ± 0.024) outperforms Plan 1
Cluster (0.782 ± 0.030) marginally but benefits from cross-HLA knowledge transfer.
For HLA-C cold-start, the Pan-HLA model (Plan 4 Cluster, 0.896 ± 0.013) far
surpasses both Plan 1 Cluster (0.403 ± 0.066) and Plan 3 fine-tuning (0.593 ±
0.101), demonstrating that pooling all HLA training data provides superior
generalization for rare alleles. Dashed line: random classifier baseline (0.5).

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Pan-HLA universal model (Plan 4) versus individual HLA-A, HLA-B, and HLA-C models
(Plan 1) across all six evaluation metrics (mean ± SD, n = 10 seeds). Six subpanels
correspond to AUROC, AUPRC, F1, Sensitivity, Specificity, and Accuracy. Within each

Figure 6  |  fig6_gray_scatter.{pdf,png,svg}  [Gray Zone Inference]
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Scatter plot of predicted binding probability versus log10(IC50) for 45,115
gray-zone peptides (IC50 100–10,000 nM) not included in binary training.
Left panel: Pan-HLA Random model (best_model_epoch_15.pth, seed 42).
Right panel: Pan-HLA Cluster model (best_model_epoch_12.pth, seed 42).
Points are colored by HLA type: blue = HLA-A (n = 32,638), orange = HLA-B
(n = 11,565), green = HLA-C (n = 912); black line = linear trend (OLS).
Vertical dotted lines mark 500 nM (clinical weak-binder threshold, pink) and
2,000 nM (non-binder cutoff in some conventions, purple); horizontal dashed line
marks P = 0.5 decision boundary. Spearman correlation between −log10(IC50) and
predicted probability: r = 0.455 (Random), r = 0.383 (Cluster), p ≈ 0 for both.
The monotonic relationship confirms that the binary Pan-HLA model learned a
continuous affinity ranking consistent with established IC50 thresholds, even
though it was never trained on gray-zone peptides.

Figure 7  |  fig7_gray_boxplot.{pdf,png,svg}  [Gray Zone Inference]
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Box plots of predicted binding probability for six IC50 bins spanning the gray
zone (100–200, 200–500, 500–1k, 1k–2k, 2k–5k, 5k–10k nM).
Left panel: Pan-HLA Random; Right panel: Pan-HLA Cluster. Boxes colored by a
green-to-red colormap (low IC50 = green/high probability, high IC50 = red/low
probability). Horizontal dashed line marks P = 0.5; sample counts shown below
each box. Pan-HLA Random bin means descend monotonically from 0.737 (100–200 nM)
to 0.226 (5k–10k nM), crossing the 0.5 boundary between the 500–1k nM
(mean = 0.519) and 1k–2k nM (mean = 0.423) bins — consistent with the
conventional 500 nM clinical binder/non-binder threshold. Pan-HLA Cluster shows
the same trend at a uniformly lower probability level (0.599 → 0.192).

Figure 8  |  fig8_gray_histogram.{pdf,png,svg}  [Gray Zone Inference]
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Predicted binding probability distributions for Pan-HLA Random model on gray-zone
peptides, stratified by HLA type. Three subpanels: HLA-A (n = 32,638), HLA-B
(n = 11,565), HLA-C (n = 912). Each histogram uses 40 bins; red dashed line marks
P = 0.5; inset text shows the percentage of peptides classified as binders (P > 0.5).
HLA-A: 49.1% predicted binders; HLA-B: 41.5%; HLA-C: 44.6%. The bimodal-to-broad
distributions reflect the gray zone's inherent ambiguity: many peptides near 500 nM
receive moderate-to-high scores (near 0.5), while those approaching 10,000 nM
receive near-zero scores. The model behaves as a soft continuous classifier even
in the unseen IC50 range.
